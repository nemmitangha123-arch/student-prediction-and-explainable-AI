## 5. Precision / recall trade-offs (from the tables above)

- **Moving the High Concern cut-off down** (for example to < 38) flags 11 students and detects 8 of
  25 (recall 0.320, precision 0.727): fewer false alarms, more
  genuinely low-scoring students missed.
- **Moving the High Concern cut-off up** (for example to < 42) flags 27 students and detects 15 of
  25 (recall 0.600, precision 0.556), compared with 13 of
  25 and 17 flagged at < 40.
- **Widening the Borderline band** raises coverage slowly and flags many more students: at ≤ 46, 23 of
  25 low scorers are flagged with 75 students flagged in total; at ≤ 48,
  23 of 25 with 120 flagged, compared with 21 of
  25 and 47 flagged at ≤ 44. For actual ≤ 44, recall
  rises from 0.434 to 0.639 (≤ 46) and 0.783 (≤ 48).
- The 25 students below 40 are few, so each additional detection moves recall by
  0.04; the 95% confidence interval for the current High Concern recall is
  0.33–0.70. Differences of one or two students between
  candidate cut-offs are within this uncertainty.

## 6. Interpretation

- The model ranks students well (ROC-AUC 0.990), but its predictions for low scorers are pulled upwards by
  about 3.2 marks on average. This is why a fixed cut-off at 40 on the predicted mark catches only
  13 of 25 students who actually scored below 40.
- The early-warning design already has a second tier: 21 of 25 students who
  actually scored below 40 are flagged as either High Concern or Borderline, while 47 students
  (2.35% of the cohort) are flagged in total.
- Of the 4 low scorers classified Lower Concern, the predictions are
  44.4, 45.7, 48.6, 50.8. Reaching them requires a Borderline
  cut-off of about 51, which would flag 201 students
  (10.05% of the cohort).
- Any cut-off chosen by looking at these tables is fitted to these 25 students. Its measured
  performance would be an optimistic estimate. An unbiased estimate for a changed threshold would need a separate
  procedure, such as nested cross-validation (choosing the threshold inside each training split and measuring it on the
  held-out fold) or a new, independent set of students.
- None of these thresholds are "optimal" in a general sense: the right balance depends on how the institution weighs a
  missed student against an unnecessary check-in, which the data cannot decide.

## 7. Recommendation

### A. Keep current thresholds
Supported by: the thresholds are a fixed, pre-defined policy (not tuned on this data), so the reported performance is an
honest out-of-fold estimate; precision is 0.765 for High Concern; and together High Concern and
Borderline flag 21 of 25 actual low scorers while flagging only
47 students (2.35%).

### B. Change thresholds
For the students who actually scored below 40, the data show a trade-off rather than a clearly better
configuration: raising the High Concern cut-off to < 42 detects 2 more of the 25 for
10 more flags, and widening Borderline to ≤ 46 or ≤ 48 flags 2 and
2 more of them respectively, for 28 and
73 more flags. These differences are within the uncertainty of a 25-student group.

The one materially different trade-off concerns the wider group who actually scored at or below 44: widening
Borderline to ≤ 46 raises their recall from 0.434 to 0.639
(17 more of 83 students; 95% CIs 0.33–0.54
and 0.53–0.73), while precision falls from 0.766 to
0.707 and the number flagged rises from 47 to 75. This would be a policy change
to the definition of the Borderline band. Because it was identified on these same out-of-fold predictions, its
performance could only be reported after the separate validation described above.

### C. Keep current thresholds and report the limitation — recommended
This is the most defensible choice given: only 25 actual < 40 students
(1.25%, severe class imbalance); a strong ranking (ROC-AUC 0.990) combined with a High Concern
recall of 0.520 (95% CI 0.33–0.70) and precision of
0.765; and the regression model's documented tendency to pull low predictions towards the average.
For early identification, the system should be described as flagging students as High Concern **or** Borderline
(21 of 25 actual low scorers flagged), with High Concern alone as the most urgent,
highest-precision tier. The limitation to report: fixed thresholds on predicted marks miss some genuinely low-scoring
students (4 of 25 were classified Lower Concern), and any threshold change, including
the wider Borderline band described in B, would need separate validation.
